Dr. Neethu S. Ravi

Researcher in computational imaging and polarimetric imaging with over seven years of experience in image reconstruction, digital super-resolution, signal processing, and computational methods for advanced imaging systems. My research focuses on integrating signal processing, imaging physics, and computational techniques to address challenges in polarization-sensitive imaging, image restoration, and resolution enhancement.

During my doctoral research at Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham, I developed novel Generalized Sampling Theorem (GST)-based digital super-resolution techniques for division-of-focal-plane polarimetric imaging, enabling spatial resolution enhancement without modifications to the underlying imaging hardware. This research has resulted in peer-reviewed publications in IEEE Signal Processing Letters and the Journal of the Optical Society of America B, and includes international research collaboration with the University of Dayton, USA.

My broader research interests include computational imaging, inverse problems, scientific machine learning, physics-informed AI, and intelligent image reconstruction.

Future Research Directions

My future research aims to develop intelligent computational imaging systems by integrating physical models of image formation with modern machine learning and scientific computing. Building upon my doctoral research in polarimetric imaging and digital super-resolution, I am interested in developing robust reconstruction methods for recovering reliable information from limited, undersampled, noisy, or degraded measurements. A major direction is Physics-Informed AI and Scientific Machine Learning for computational imaging, where physical constraints, imaging models, and signal-processing principles are incorporated into data-driven reconstruction frameworks. I am also interested in AI-assisted super-resolution, inverse problems, spatiotemporal modelling, and multimodal imaging, with applications in polarimetric and computational imaging, autonomous sensing, remote observation, biomedical and scientific imaging, and industrial inspection.

PhD Research
GST-Based Digital Super-Resolution for Division-of-Focal-Plane Polarimetric Imaging
Division-of-focal-plane (DoFP) polarimeters acquire polarization information by sampling four polarizer orientations across the sensor, inherently reducing spatial resolution. This project developed a novel Generalized Sampling Theorem (GST)-based digital super-resolution framework that reconstructs high-resolution polarimetric images from multiple low-resolution observations — recovering spatial detail and preserving polarization information without any hardware modification. The work investigates robust reconstruction under shift-estimation uncertainty and validates results on experimental microgrid polarimeter imagery in collaboration with the University of Dayton, USA.

M.Tech Thesis Project  ·  Public Repository
Cardiac Disorder Detection from ECG Signals Using ANN and Fuzzy Logic
Developed an improved method to detect common cardiac disorders — including arrhythmias and conduction abnormalities — from ECG signals. The approach combines Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) for pattern recognition with Fuzzy Logic for handling uncertainty in clinical decision-making, improving classification accuracy over conventional single-model methods. A modular classification approach was also proposed for finer disorder categorisation.

About

I am a researcher in computational imaging and polarimetric imaging with over seven years of experience in developing image reconstruction and resolution enhancement algorithms for advanced imaging systems. My research focuses on bridging signal processing, imaging physics, and computational methods to address challenges in polarization-sensitive imaging, image restoration, and information extraction from complex visual data.

During my doctoral research at Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham, I developed novel digital super-resolution techniques for division-of-focal-plane polarimeters based on the Generalized Sampling Theorem, enabling improved spatial resolution without modifications to imaging hardware. My work has resulted in publications in leading journals including IEEE Signal Processing Letters and the Journal of the Optical Society of America B, and has been conducted in collaboration with researchers at the University of Dayton, USA.

My broader interests lie in computational imaging, inverse problems, machine learning for imaging systems, and the development of intelligent imaging solutions that combine physical modeling with data-driven approaches. I am particularly interested in translating fundamental research into practical technologies for scientific, industrial, and real-world imaging applications.

Research Focus

Digital Super-Resolution

Polarimetric imaging provides information beyond conventional intensity imaging by measuring the polarization state of light, enabling enhanced characterization of materials, surfaces, and scenes. This additional information has applications in remote sensing, surveillance, biomedical imaging, industrial inspection, and autonomous sensing. However, many polarimetric imaging systems suffer from reduced spatial resolution due to the way polarization information is acquired at the sensor level.

My research focuses on developing computational imaging techniques to overcome these limitations and improve the quality of polarimetric imagery. In particular, I developed a novel Generalized Sampling Theorem (GST)-based digital super-resolution framework that reconstructs high-resolution images from multiple low-resolution observations. The work combines signal processing theory, mathematical modeling, and image reconstruction techniques to recover spatial detail while preserving polarization information.

Through this research, I aim to advance the capabilities of next-generation imaging systems by integrating computational methods with imaging physics, enabling improved image quality and information extraction without requiring modifications to the underlying hardware.

Future Research

Future Research

My future research aims to advance the capabilities of next-generation imaging systems through the integration of computational imaging, intelligent reconstruction methods, and emerging data-driven technologies. Building upon my work in polarimetric imaging and image reconstruction, I am interested in exploring approaches that improve image quality, information recovery, and decision-making in challenging imaging environments.

A key objective is to develop scalable and adaptable imaging frameworks that combine physical understanding of image formation with modern computational techniques. These efforts are directed toward creating robust solutions for scientific imaging, autonomous sensing, remote observation, industrial inspection, and other applications where extracting reliable information from limited or degraded measurements is critical.

Through interdisciplinary collaboration, I seek to contribute to the development of advanced imaging technologies that are both theoretically grounded and practically deployable across a wide range of real-world scenarios.
